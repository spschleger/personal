# Mine stopping and seal platform — initial feasibility

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: exploratory research, not an approved design or construction instruction. No supplier, engineer or regulator contacted; no costs or test availability verified.

## Shane's concept

Shane works in underground coal mining and wants to explore his own Australian rated stopping/seal designs. Desired platform: high-volume wet spraying, or two sprayed permanent form walls with a pumped plug between them, ideally using the same product and pump for both operations. No commitment to develop has been made.

## Initial conclusion

Worth testing as an installation-system improvement; novelty and performance are not established. Aquacrete markets DustBoss wet-mix ventilation material and a wet-spray unit also capable of pumping other grouts and foaming products. This does not establish that its same formulation performs both roles.[5][6] Minova's 2020 brochure describes sprayed stoppings tested at 2/5 psi and Tekseal/Meshblock seals tested at 20/50 psi.[3]

## Approval and acceptance

Queensland Coal Mining Safety and Health Regulation 2017 section 350 and Schedule 4 require application-specific design criteria; section 352 requires a construction, installation, use and maintenance SOP. Schedule 4 specifies 14 kPa for panel-system stoppings/overcasts/regulators, 35 kPa for main-system equivalents and type B seals, 140 kPa for type C, 345 kPa for type D and 70 kPa for type E. These are not a permission to use a chosen class anywhere.[1]

NSW Regulation 2022 section 71 requires seal risk management considering flammable gas, ignition sources, pressure piling and expected duration in the installed condition; section 73 also addresses goaf/sealed workings.[4] Do not import the Queensland classification as NSW approval. A universal Australian product approval process has not been established by this research. The exact certification, test evidence, registration/notification and site acceptance requirements must be agreed with a competent mine-seal designer and the target mine, with regulator clarification where required.

## Proposed development gates — recommendations, not a statutory checklist

1. Select one state, one mine use case, one target rating and a bounded roadway/ground envelope. Benchmark crew hours, material, logistics, cure time and rework against the incumbent.
2. Engage an experienced mine-seal structural/blast designer with geotechnical input and a materials formulator. Agree the design basis, independent review and evidence/acceptance plan before formulation spending. Resolve engineering registration requirements, including Queensland RPEQ applicability.
3. Screen one-base-material/two-application-mode feasibility. Treat identical finished mixture as a hypothesis, not a constraint worth sacrificing performance for. Test spray build/adhesion and pump/flow behaviour separately. Do not vary water informally to get flow.
4. Run controlled surface material and pump trials, then a representative mock-up. Establish measurable placement output, stoppage/restart behaviour, early-age properties, durability and inspection methods.
5. Engineer the temporary construction state and final seal separately. Test candidate form walls against the intended fresh-fill loading. Resolve roof contact, trapped air, interfaces, penetrations, ground movement and leakage. No safety-critical underground trial before agreed authorisation and controls.
6. Execute the agreed structural/explosion validation programme with an appropriate specialist facility. Agree pressure-time history, leakage acceptance and applicability of modelling/static tests in advance. Historical test sites in brochures are not verified available today.
7. Produce a bounded design family, drawings, material specification and batch controls, pump/application limits, installation hold points, acceptance tests, training, as-built records and change control. Obtain mine-specific acceptance and required regulatory steps before service.

## Engineering questions to prove

These are proposed review questions, not verified conclusions about a formulation. Can one base mix stick and build in spray mode yet fill completely in placement mode? What changes between modes and how are they controlled? Can the young skins withstand the proposed filling process? Is the final capacity carried by the core, skins or a validated composite? How are weak/wet strata, roof gaps, shrinkage, segregation, heat, penetrations and convergence handled? What proves installed quality without destructive inspection of every seal?

Recommendation: assess the two-wall plug route first for the higher-rated concept, without assuming it will outperform a monolithic sprayed alternative. Aim for one material supply and one pumping platform with controlled operating modes; allow a second compatible formulation if the single-product constraint worsens installed cost or assurance.

## Immediate questions

Is NSW or Queensland the first market? Which current product/pump and installation sequence is the benchmark? Is the initial objective a stopping or a higher-rated goaf seal? Does one product permit a controlled nozzle additive? What roadway envelope and desired actual placement output are involved?
