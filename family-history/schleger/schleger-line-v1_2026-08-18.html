1THE FINDING

The "Austrian great-grandfather" is real — but he's your great-great-grandfather, and he called himself Hungarian

Every Schleger in Australia whose records I could reach descends from one man: Carl Schleger, who anglicised to Francis Charles ("Frank") Schleger. He was born about 1851, a subject of the Kingdom of Hungary inside the Austro-Hungarian Empire, worked as a baker, and reached Maryborough, Queensland at the end of the 1870s. He married an Irish immigrant, Mary O'Meara, in June 1879, took the oath of allegiance at Bundaberg in 1886, and farmed at New Bundaberg / Burnett Heads until his death in December 1930.

His own newspaper obituary calls him "a native of Austria" and his 1929 golden-wedding write-up says "Mr Schleger from Austria" — but his sworn 1886 oath and the Supreme Court register both record his country as Hungary. Both are true at once if he came from the German-speaking western fringe of Hungary — the strip that became Austria's Burgenland in 1921. A Bundaberg-based Schleger researcher independently gives the family's origin as Eisenstadt (Kismarton), the Burgenland capital. That is exactly such a place.

6THE OLD COUNTRY

Where the name lives in Austria — and why "Hungary" fits

The surname

Schleger is genuinely rare: roughly 480 bearers worldwide (Germany 133, USA 97, Brazil 96, Austria 60, Australia 27). Austria has the highest per-capita frequency of any country. The scholarly etymology (Digitales Familiennamenwörterbuch Deutschlands) gives it as either a variant of Schleier — a maker or seller of veils and head-cloths — or an umlaut-less spelling of Schläger (a brawler, or one who lives by a forest clearing). The popular "hammer-striker / smith" gloss on commercial name sites is not supported by the scholarly source. B

Inside Austria the name is confined to Vienna and eastern Lower Austria: phone-book and 2005 name-map data put the largest cluster in Vienna, then Bruck an der Leitha district (Wolfsthal, Hainburg, Bad Deutsch-Altenburg, Mannersdorf) and the Weinviertel (Ziersdorf, Wolkersdorf). Zero in the western provinces. A (dataset)

The Habsburg-era paper trail (ANNO newspaper archive)

  • Wolfsthal, Bruck a.d. Leitha: Josef Schleger, Kleinhäusler, d. 31 Jan 1920 "in his 70th year" (b. c.1850, veteran of the 1878 Bosnia campaign); Johann Schleger, house-owner, d. 1928 aged 72; Infantryman Johann Schleger, b. Wolfsthal 1894, wounded 1915. A
  • Vienna: Wenzel Schleger, grinder, 1787; Adalbert Schleger, master tailor, d. 1873; Floridsdorf Schlegers 1925. A
  • Bohemia (Sudeten German): Saaz, Laun, Pilsen, Brüx miners 1873–1916; footballer Walter Schleger b. Prague 1929. A
  • Hungary proper: thin traces at Temesvár, Neusatz, Budapest 1862–1884. A

How it hangs together. Wolfsthal sits on the Danube directly on the old Hungarian border, opposite Pressburg (Bratislava). Eisenstadt/Kismarton is 40 km south of it — German-speaking, in the Kingdom of Hungary until 1921, in Austria since. A Carl Schleger born there about 1851 would swear "Hungary" to a Queensland magistrate in 1886 and be "from Austria" to his family and the Bundaberg press. A Josef Schleger born c.1850 in Wolfsthal is a plausible brother or cousin. C — this is the reconciling hypothesis, not yet a documented link.

What points toward it

  • No registered birth. There is no Queensland birth for a Rose Jackson c.1893 to a Robert Jackson and a Millie/Amelia — every other Robert-Jackson family of the period is registered with the mother's full maiden name. Unregistered births were the norm for Aboriginal families in 1890s Queensland. C
  • The mother's name. "Millie Jackson" — a single given name carrying the husband's surname, no maiden name — is exactly how Aboriginal women were commonly entered on Queensland certificates by informants who did not know, or were not asked for, a maiden name. It is also, to be fair, how a poor informant might record any grandmother whose maiden name they never knew. C
  • Place. Rockhampton is Darumbal country; the town and its railway camps had a large Aboriginal and South Sea Islander working population from the 1890s. Rose Jackson lived her whole recorded life there. B
  • Family knowledge. Your aunt Carmel Schleger works publicly as a counsellor and Indigenous consultant in SE Queensland — the family may already hold this history in a form no archive does. B

What would settle it

  • Rose Johnstone's 1974 death certificate (Qld BDM, C7759/1974, ~$25) — birthplace, parents, informant. And the 1919 marriage certificate (C2254/1919) — her age, birthplace, parents, and whether the marriage needed the Protector's permission (which, under the 1897 Act, marriages of Aboriginal women did — a decisive tell if present).
  • Queensland's Community & Personal Histories unit (Dept of Treaty, Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander Partnerships) holds the Protector's files, exemption certificates and removal registers 1897–1970s, indexed by name. Descendants can request a free family-history search — Robert Jackson, Millie, and Rose Jackson of Rockhampton are the names to give them.
  • Frederick Jason Johnstone's family (George Johnstone & Harriett Ann Curtis; sons Thomas, Henry, William John, George Vincent, Frederick — all dying at Rockhampton 1916–69) has no Queensland births or marriage, so they came from elsewhere, probably NSW; that side is a separate question and not where the Aboriginal thread runs on present evidence.

Bottom line: the record is consistent with Aboriginal ancestry through Rose Jackson's mother "Millie", and I found nothing that contradicts it — but nothing I could reach today proves it. Two certificates and one letter to Community & Personal Histories would.

9OPEN QUESTIONS

What is not yet proven, and how to close it

  • Exact birthplace. "Hungary" (sworn) + "Austria" (family) + "Eisenstadt" (a researcher's listing) is strong but not documentary. The 1879 marriage certificate (Qld BDM, C741/1879) will state his birthplace and age; the 1930 death certificate (C3513/1930) will state birthplace, years in Queensland and parents. Both are ~A$25 each from Qld BDM. That is the single highest-value next step.
  • Arrival. No passenger-list entry found under any spelling. Once birthplace is fixed, check Hamburg/Bremen departure lists and the full-fare Scottish Hero manifest at QSA.
  • The Austrian parish. Eisenstadt's Catholic registers (Diocese of Eisenstadt) and Wolfsthal/Hainburg's (Archdiocese of Vienna) are online at Matricula; GenTeam.at (free registration) indexes Lower Austrian marriages and WWI casualty lists. Search for a Karl/Carl Schleger baptised c.1851 to Carl Schleger & Katharina Frank.
  • Your own steps — closed. Dean & Judy → Shane confirmed; Alphonsus Vincent "Phonse" is your grandfather, Alphonso Leo your great-grandfather, and Carl/Frank the Hungarian-born baker your great-great-grandfather. The chain from Eisenstadt(?) to you is now continuous in the record from 1879 onward.
  • Yola Rose's side. Buy Rose Johnstone's 1974 death and 1919 marriage certificates; lodge a family-history request with Qld Community & Personal Histories for Robert Jackson / Millie / Rose Jackson of Rockhampton. That is the route to documenting (or ruling out) Aboriginal ancestry.
