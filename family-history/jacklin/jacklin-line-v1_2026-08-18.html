1THE FINDING

Six generations back on your mother's side are now in the record — and "North" is a grandmother's maiden name

Your grandmother Margaret Elizabeth Jacklin was born a Ward, at Tambo Upper near Bairnsdale, Victoria, about 1927, the younger daughter of a soldier-settler dairy farmer. In April 1952 she married Dr Desmond John Jacklin of Mackay at Bairnsdale, and moved north. Desmond was the elder son of Abraham "Abe" Jacklin — a Mackay hotel-keeper, WWI corporal, taxi and picture-theatre man — and Annie Agnes Taylor, eldest daughter of the Irish couple who ran the Queensland Hotel and the Theatre Royal in Mackay. Abe's parents, Thomas Jacklin and Fanny Margaret Holmes, came out from Louth, Lincolnshire on the sailing ship Hannah Landells in September 1883 and farmed cane at North Eton for the rest of their lives.

Your great-uncle North Jacklin (Abe's younger son, b. c.1932, still living) is John North Jacklin — the North Jacklin of the North Jacklin Motor Group in Mackay, which he founded in April 1961 with his wife Judy after a decade at Fields' Motors. He carries the maiden name of his grandmother, Mary North, Mrs John Taylor of the Queensland Hotel. The Mackay Taylor cousins are her descendants.

3GRAND-
PARENTS

Dr Desmond John Jacklin (1926–1981) & Margaret Elizabeth Ward (c.1927–2025)

Desmond was Mackay-born (his 1981 death entry gives 1926), the elder son of Abe and Annie Agnes of Gordon Street. He is first seen aged one, in a white satin suit, as page boy at his uncle George Taylor's wedding in December 1927. He won a State scholarship in 1939, went south to Nudgee College in Brisbane (a "high pass" in the 1942 Senior, and the college 100-yards), and graduated MB BS from the University of Queensland in the class of November 1948. By 1953 he was senior resident medical officer and acting medical superintendent of the Mackay District Hospital; he resigned in November 1953 and opened his own practice at 73 Evans Avenue, North Mackay on 25 January 1954. He was a keen fisherman — the "Angling Antics" column reports his catches. He died on 9 September 1981, aged 55; his estate file is at Queensland State Archives (ITM946943).

Margaret was the younger daughter of Charles William and Maud Ward of Tambo Upper. She appears in the Bairnsdale paper from childhood — a Sunday-school concert item in 1933, a letter to the Melbourne Sun's children's page in 1935 written in the voice of the family pig ("I suppose I will be sent to market some day"), the ladies' race at the 1944 gymkhana, and as bridesmaid at her sister Mary's wedding in December 1944. Her engagement to "Dr D. Jacklin" was announced in Mackay on 28 November 1951 (cocktail party at the Hotel Ambassador — pale-blue crepe, solitaire diamond) and in Melbourne on 14 December; Tambo Upper friends presented her with a silver tea service on 8 April 1952, and she married Desmond at Bairnsdale on 19 April 1952. By October 1955 "Mrs Des Jacklin, of Mackay, with her husband and two children" was driving to Melbourne to be matron of honour at a Hobart wedding. She died in 2025 — the Queensland probate index lists Margaret Elizabeth Jacklin, late of Beaconsfield (a Mackay suburb), 3 October 2025.

"In honor of Miss Margaret Ward, of Tambo Upper, who is to be married to Dr. Desmond Jacklin, of Mackay (Q.), on April 19, at Bairnsdale, thirty-five friends gathered at the home of Mr. and Mrs. L. G. Moon … Mrs. Johnston conveyed to Miss Ward their very best wishes for her future happiness, and asked her to accept a silver tea service and tray for use in her future home."Bairnsdale Advertiser, 17 April 1952 (Trove 269227724)

North Jacklin

Desmond's younger brother John North Jacklin followed him to Nudgee (GPS high-jump and swimming record-holder 1947–49), came home to become the Mackay Surf Life Saving Club's junior beach-sprint champion (1950–52) and club captain (1953–54, credited with a beach rescue in December 1953), and started at Fields' Motors around 1951 — Dr and Mrs D. Jacklin hosted his farewell at their Bridge Road home in September 1952 before a Townsville posting. He opened the North Jacklin business (B.M.C. franchise, rented premises) on 20 April 1961; the group marked sixty years in 2021 and credits "company founders North & Judy Jacklin".

4GREAT-GRAND-
PARENTS

Abraham "Abe" Jacklin (1891–1962) & Ann Agnes Taylor (1894–1971)

Abe was born at North Eton on 9 October 1891, the second surviving son of Thomas and Fanny (an earlier Abraham had lived eight months). He served in the AIF — the casualty lists carry "Cpl. Abe Jacklin, Mackay" wounded in December 1917 and gassed (second occasion) in June 1918; his B2455 service dossier at the National Archives has not yet been pulled. Home again, he ran a hire car in Mackay (suing passengers for unpaid fares in 1924), then went into hotels: the Albion at Walkerston to 1926, the Cremorne on the North Side 1927–29, premises in Gordon Street (Jacklin's Flats) in the 1930s and the Carmila hotel in 1935; by 1946 he was a picture-theatre exhibitor. He was co-executor of his father's £3,249 estate in 1931 and, with his brother-in-law Jack Taylor, physically detained a thief in the 1931 Queensland Hotel robbery. He died on 18 March 1962.

Annie Agnes was the eldest daughter of John and Mary Taylor of the Queensland Hotel, Wood Street; the couple married at St Patrick's, Mackay, on 30 September 1924 (bridesmaid her sister "Tot", best man his brother Harry, honeymoon in the Blue Mountains) and lived at the hotel with her mother in the early 1930s. She was a Ladies' Suburban Bowling Club regular through the 1940s and died on 19 July 1971. Only two children have been found: Desmond John (1926) and John North (c.1932).

"Jacklin — Taylor. — A quiet but very pretty wedding was celebrated at St. Patrick's Roman Catholic Church on Tuesday, 30th September, the contracting parties being Annie Agnes, eldest daughter of Mr. and Mrs. J. Taylor, Queensland Hotel, Mackay, and Abe, second eldest son of Mr. and Mrs. Jacklin, Eton."Daily Mercury (Mackay), 7 October 1924 (Trove 168111881)
5THE
IMMIGRANTS

Thomas Jacklin (1857–1931) & Fanny Margaret Holmes (c.1858–1933) — Louth, Lincolnshire → North Eton

Thomas's obituary is unusually specific: born in Lincolnshire in 1857, native town Louth, he "arrived in Mackay with his wife, who is still hale and hearty, on September 13, 1883, by the sailing ship Hannah Landells". His first job was helping load Mrs Mitchell's piano onto Pugsley's wagon — Mrs Mitchell being the future Dame Nellie Melba, then living at Marian. He worked as a farm hand at Eton Vale, North Eton, the Barry Estate, Victoria Mill, Dalrymple's "Pill Box" and Farleigh, was "regarded as one of the best ploughmen in the district", leased land from J. Antoney to grow cane for North Eton Mill's first crushing, bought freehold at Double Peak which he named Louth Park, and ended up owning the North Eton block he had once leased. He was a prominent rifleman. His death registration names his parents as Abraham Jacklin and Elizabeth Skipworth. His brother William (d. 1942, parents "Abraham & Maria Skipworth") settled at Ipswich and founded the Gatton / Glenore Grove Jacklins.

Fanny Margaret was the eldest daughter of Langley Holmes (b. c.1844, d. Mackay 27 Apr 1924) and Fanny Shepherd (d. 31 Jan 1918 aged 79, daughter of Thomas Grant Shepherd). Her parents and siblings followed her to Mackay on the Decca in 1887 and farmed at Woodford on the Pioneer; her brother John Jarvis Holmes worked two and a half years alongside "his brother-in-law, the late Mr T. Jacklin" at North Eton. Fanny died on 24 January 1933.

"Born in Lincolnshire in 1857 he arrived in Mackay with his wife, who is still hale and hearty, on September 13, 1883, by the sailing ship Hannah Landells. The first job he received in Mackay was to assist in loading Mrs. Mitchell's (afterwards Dame Nellie Melba) piano on to Pugsley's wagon … He purchased freehold land at Double Peak, which he called Louth Park, after his native town in England."Daily Mercury (Mackay), 22 October 1931 (Trove 170295896)
6THE IRISH
SIDE

John Taylor (d. 1925) & Mary North (c.1863–1937) — Queensland Hotel & Theatre Royal, Mackay

Mary North was born in Ireland about 1863 to Patrick North and Agnes Cooney, reached Mackay about 1888 and on 5 January 1893 married John Taylor, an Irish-born farmer (parents Robert Taylor and Anne Stokes) then at Homebush and the Six-Mile. They moved into town in 1905 and "engaged in hotel and theatre ownership": John held the Queensland Hotel licence until it passed to Mary in 1915, and owned the Theatre Royal. John died on 2 October 1925 after a stroke; Mary ran the old Queensland Hotel until its demolition in 1936, made four trips home to Ireland (1900, 1907, 1914 and 1932 for the Eucharistic Congress in Dublin), collapsed at a Saturday matinee at the Theatre Royal and died the next day, 25 July 1937, aged 74. Seven children survived them, including Dr H. J. Taylor (medical superintendent at Townsville, later practising in Mackay), Jack, Bob and Jim Taylor, and Mesdames Jacklin, Ryan (Sarina) and Phillips (Brisbane) — the source of the Mackay Taylor cousins.

7THE
GIPPSLAND SIDE

Charles William Ward (1895–1962) & Maud Frances Spunner (1895–1988) — Tambo Upper

Charles William Ward was born in 1895 at Cunninghame — the old name for Lakes Entrance — to William Ward and Catherine Cunningham, who had married that year. He served in the First AIF ("his father fought in the Great War", said the 1941 farewell to his son) and in September 1921 the Closer Settlement Board allotted him a block in the parish of Tambo under the Discharged Soldiers' Settlement Act. He built a dairy herd there: the Bairnsdale District Herd Testing Association's secretary and treasurer for many years, its vice-president in 1934, and owner of the district's best herd in 1937; president of the Bruthen RSL sub-branch until 1942, a founder-member of the Bruthen Rural Fire Brigade, and Tambo Upper secretary of the Victorian Dairyfarmers' Association. He died at Bairnsdale in 1962 aged 67. Maud Frances Spunner, born at Kensington in Melbourne in 1895 to William Henry Spunner (of a Creswick family) and Mary Ann Agnes Corkhill, lost her mother at eight; she married Charles in 1919, ran the Ladies' Victory Club and CWA at Bruthen, judged the schoolwork at the Bruthen show, and died at Lakes Entrance in 1988 aged 92. Their children were William Charles (1920, "Gunner W. Ward"), George Victor (1921–1963), Mary Frances (1923, Mrs Morrison) and Margaret Elizabeth — who is missing from her grandfather Spunner's November 1926 death notice ("Billy, George and Mary Ward, Tambo Upper"), which pins her birth to 1927 as you remembered.

Where the Wards came from

One more generation is now in the record. William Ward's obituary (Bairnsdale Advertiser, 29 Jan 1953) says he was born at Dunolly on the central Victorian goldfields, "came to Bairnsdale in his youth", married Catherine Cunningham of Swan Reach, took up "a selection of virgin bush country" at Lake Tyers and farmed it for many years, spent his later years in Melbourne and then at Picnic Point, and died there on 18 January 1953 aged 85, his wife having predeceased him — one son and three daughters (one deceased). The Victorian index has exactly one matching birth: William Ward, 1867, Eddington (the next village to Dunolly), son of Henry Ward and Anne Burns; his 1953 death entry gives his mother as "Annie Burns". Henry Ward married Anne Byrns in Victoria in 1855, and their children were registered at Prahran (1857), Maryborough (1861) and Eddington (1865–73) — the mother's maiden name wobbling between Byrnes/Burns and Cavannagh/Kavanagh, both Irish. Anne died at Eddington in 1887 aged 53. So the Wards were on the goldfields by 1855 and Australian-born from 1857; where Henry and Anne themselves were born is not yet established.

Catherine's people are the Cunninghams of the Tambo: Thomas Cunningham (d. Swan Reach, April 1915, buried Johnsonville) and Margaret née Murphy, who died at Swan Reach in November 1936 aged 97 — her notice in The Age says she was born at Kiandra, New South Wales, that the couple "crossed the border in 1867 and settled on the Tambo River" and reared six sons and four daughters. Catherine, born about 1867–72, would be one of those daughters — which explains why no Victorian birth exists for her. Her own death record has not been found, so that last link is a strong inference rather than a proof.
