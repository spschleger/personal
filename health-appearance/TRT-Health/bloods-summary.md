## CV markers — what they mean + my baseline (2026-06-22)

**Core idea:** atherosclerosis (heart attack, most strokes) is a *particle-trapping* disease. Cholesterol travels in protein-wrapped spheres; the harmful ones each carry exactly **one ApoB protein**. Plaque forms from the *number* of these particles lodging in the artery wall — not the cholesterol they carry. Trucks-in-a-tunnel: what clogs it is how many trucks, not the cargo per truck. This is why **ApoB (counts particles) beats LDL-C (measures cargo)** as a risk marker — they can disagree, and ApoB is the one that tracks events.

Four roughly-independent CV axes:

| Marker | My value (22 Jun 26) | Axis | Reference / target | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ApoB** | **0.7 g/L** | Particle *number* — the headline lipid risk marker | <0.8 good; ~0.65 optimal | Low. Good. |
| **ApoA1** | **1.2 g/L** | Main HDL protein — "clearance" side | Men ≳1.2 | Adequate; the weakest link |
| **ApoB/A1 ratio** | **0.6** | Single risk ratio (in vs cleared) | <0.7 good; ~0.5 excellent | Just above excellent |
| **Lp(a)** | **<19 nmol/L** | A *genetic*, lifelong-fixed, extra-sticky + pro-clotting ApoB particle | <100 nmol/L (<80th %ile) | Low — **good genetic hand; test once, don't repeat** |
| **hsCRP** | **0.73 mg/L** | Arterial-wall *inflammation* (independent of lipids) | <1.0 = low-risk tertile | Low |

**Key points for me:**
- **ApoB is the one to track long-term.** Mine (0.7) is low *and concordant* with LDL 2.4 — no hidden discordance where LDL looks fine but particle count is secretly high.
- **Lp(a) is a one-and-done test** — ~90% genetic, basically unmoved by diet/exercise/statins, stays flat for life. Mine is low → an all-clear on a risk factor I otherwise can't control. No need to re-measure.
- **hsCRP caveat:** non-specific acute-phase marker — a hard session, infection, or the recent epistaxis/stress can transiently bump it. 0.73 is low even with that noise.
- **HDL/ApoA1 is my soft spot** — fine, not great. Zone 2 cardio is one of the few genuine levers to raise it.

**Net:** 3 of 4 axes (particle burden, genetic, inflammation) are genuinely strong; clearance side is adequate. **My CV risk is driven far more by BP + haematocrit/viscosity than by lipids** — the lipid/inflammation engine is in good shape, so the levers that matter for me are the candesartan + CPAP + Hct work, not statins.

**TRT watch:** TRT can erode HDL/ApoA1 and nudge ApoB over time. They look good now → having ApoB + ApoA1 in the tracker means I'll catch drift early instead of inferring it from LDL.
