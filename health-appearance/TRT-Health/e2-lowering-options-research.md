# Lowering E2 on TRT — Sustainable / OTC Options (Evidence-Graded)

**Compiled:** 2026-06-30 · evidence-graded research review
**Goal (Shane):** Lower E2 *a bit* from ~207 pmol/L — not to fix symptoms (asymptomatic), but to (1) compare how he feels lower, and (2) create headroom to increase test dose / trial hCG knowing he can return to ~200 and feel fine. Prefers OTC/sustainable (DIM etc.). Expects the recomp/leaning-out to help.

---

## Framing (the honest context)
- You're **asymptomatic at 207**, and **E2 is cardioprotective** (favourable lipids + endothelial function) — relevant given HDL is your weakest CV marker. So the *sound* reason here is the headroom-for-higher-androgens logic, not the number itself.
- **The biggest, most sustainable lever is fat loss — your own instinct is correct.** Supplements are a distant second, and most OTC "estrogen blockers" are weak or useless. The only *reliable* lever is a pharma AI, which you're (reasonably) avoiding.

---

## Tier 1 — Lifestyle levers (best evidence, sustainable)

**Fat loss / leaning out — STRONG mechanism, modest magnitude.**
Adipose tissue is the **major site of aromatase** (the enzyme converting T→E2); ~85% of a man's estrogen comes from peripheral aromatization, fat being a key source. Leaning out genuinely reduces aromatase capacity → lower E2. *Caveat:* the weight-loss→E2 effect is "at best modest," and it's biggest in obese men — you're not obese, so the marginal drop from leaning out further may be modest. Still the most legitimate, sustainable lever, and it's already your plan. **Evidence: strong mechanism, modest effect.**

**Alcohol reduction — legitimate + double benefit.** Alcohol raises aromatase activity and impairs hepatic estrogen clearance. Cutting it lowers E2 *and* removes your #1 gout trigger. **Evidence: moderate.** Two-for-one for you.

---

## Tier 2 — Boron (the best OTC supplement bet)

**This is the one OTC option with real human data showing actual serum E2 reduction.**
- Naghii et al. 2011 and similar: **10 mg/day for 1 week → estradiol down ~39% (42.3 → 25.8 pg/mL), free T up ~25%.** Dose range 6–10 mg/day.
- Mechanism: partly via **lowering SHBG** (raises free fraction) plus some estrogen effect.
- **Caveats specific to you:**
  1. Studies were in **non-TRT men** — whether the E2 drop replicates on exogenous-T-driven E2 is unproven.
  2. It **lowers SHBG → raises free T** — your free T is already high and is your **erythropoiesis (Hct) driver**. So boron could nudge Hct up. **Monitor Hct if you trial it.**
  3. Small studies (n≈8), short duration, total-T changes inconsistent.
- Cheap, OTC, well-tolerated, sustainable. UL ~20 mg/day; 6–10 mg is the studied range. **Evidence: moderate (best of the OTC field). Worth an n=1 trial — with Hct monitoring.**

---

## Tier 3 — DIM / I3C (popular but weak — and I'd be cautious for you)

Your first thought, but the evidence doesn't back it and there are cautions that matter *for you specifically*:
- **Mechanism is metabolite-shifting, not E2-lowering.** DIM shifts estrogen *metabolism* (favours 2-OH over 16-OH metabolites) — it changes the *profile* more than it lowers *total* serum E2. Human evidence for actually dropping serum E2 in men is **insufficient**.
- **AR antagonism:** DIM can act as an androgen-receptor antagonist — i.e. it can blunt the very androgen signal you're trying to increase.
- **Fertility caution:** animal data shows impaired spermatogenesis / reproductive toxicity at higher doses — relevant given your fertility goals (strongly fertile pre-TRT baseline you want to protect).
- **Evidence: weak for total-E2 reduction; real downsides for your situation.** **Not recommended for you** despite the hype.

---

## Tier 4 — Calcium D-Glucarate (plausible, unproven in humans)

- Mechanism: inhibits gut **beta-glucuronidase** → less estrogen reabsorbed → more excreted. Animal data ~25% estradiol reduction.
- **Not replicated in humans; no robust evidence it lowers serum E2 in men.** Safe, sound biochemistry, but practically unproven.
- **Evidence: none in humans.** Optional low-expectation stack item, not a primary lever.

---

## Skip list (don't waste money)

- **Chrysin** — potent aromatase inhibitor *in vitro*, but **oral bioavailability is 0.003–0.02%** → plasma levels ~100× too low to inhibit aromatase; human studies confirm **no effect** on E2/T. Useless orally despite heavy marketing.
- **Grape seed / other flavonoids (luteolin, apigenin)** — same bioavailability problem; in-vitro only, unreliable in humans.
- **Zinc as an "aromatase inhibitor"** — no solid human evidence. Correcting a deficiency supports healthy T, but if you're replete it won't lower E2. Ensure adequacy, don't treat as an E2 lever.

---

## The reliable-but-avoided option: micro-dose anastrozole

The **only** lever that *reliably* lowers E2 is a pharma AI. For your specific use-case — controlling E2 *if you push test dose up or trial hCG* — a **micro-dose (e.g. 0.25–0.5 mg anastrozole 1–2×/week, PRN)** is what's actually used, because supplements are too weak to counter a deliberate androgen increase. Downsides: crash risk (over-suppression → joint pain, low libido, worse lipids), and losing E2's cardioprotection. You're avoiding it for sustainability — fair — but know that for the "headroom when increasing androgens" goal, a tiny PRN AI is more targeted than any daily supplement. Keep it in the back pocket; don't run it daily/preventively.

---

## n=1 experiment design (so you can actually "compare")

1. **Baseline:** E2 207 (22 Jun) is your starting point. Re-test E2 + free T + SHBG + **Hct** together.
2. **One variable at a time** (your usual rigour): e.g. **boron 10 mg/day for 4–6 weeks**, then re-test the same panel + note how you feel (libido, mood, joints, sleep, water retention).
3. **Mind the confounder:** the recomp/leaning-out *also* lowers E2 — if you're cutting AND on boron, you won't know which did it. **Sequence them**, or accept a combined read.
4. **Watch Hct** — if boron drops SHBG and lifts free T, Hct may tick up (your watch-item).
5. Don't stack everything at once — you'll learn nothing about what works.

---

## Bottom line
- **Most sustainable real levers: the recomp (fat loss) + cutting alcohol** — both already on your radar, both evidence-based, alcohol-cut doubles as gout protection.
- **Best OTC supplement: boron 6–10 mg/day** — the only one with human E2-lowering data — trialled n=1 with Hct monitoring.
- **Skip DIM** (weak + AR-antagonism + fertility caution), chrysin, grape seed, zinc-as-AI.
- **For headroom when actually increasing test/hCG:** supplements won't be strong enough — a PRN micro-dose AI is the real tool, used sparingly, if you go there.

## Addendum — the "others" (grey-market AIs, food levers, pharma alternatives)

**Arimistane (androsta-3,5-diene-7,17-dione) — works, but it's a grey-market drug, not a clean supplement.**
This is the big one people in TRT/bodybuilding circles mean by "natural AI." Unlike DIM/chrysin, it **actually works** — it's a **suicide (irreversible) aromatase inhibitor, same mechanism class as exemestane** — and at 50–75 mg/day for 4–8 weeks reliably lowers E2 + modestly raises free T. **BUT:** the **FDA has ruled it is NOT a legal dietary ingredient** — it's effectively an *unapproved steroidal drug sold (illegally) as a supplement*, with no safety data, no quality control, and a "cortisol modulator" effect. So it is **not** the clean/sustainable OTC thing you want — it's the same drug class as a pharma AI but unregulated and unmeasured. **Verdict: if you actually want an AI, use a pharma one (dosed precisely, quality-controlled) — don't use the grey-market analog of the same thing.** (Same applies to **6-OXO**, an older OTC steroidal AI — raised DHT, somewhat suppressive, now superseded. Skip.)

**White button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus) — the genuine *food* aromatase inhibitor.**
Real, dose-dependent aromatase inhibition (in-vitro + animal + human breast-cancer-prevention data; the active bits are C18 unsaturated fatty acids). Human trials used >50% free-E2 reduction as a response marker — but in *postmenopausal women*, not TRT men, so the effect size for you is unknown and likely modest. Upside: it's **food, benign, sustainable** — eating white button mushrooms regularly is a zero-downside dietary lever. **Evidence: moderate mechanism; unproven magnitude in men. Low-cost, low-risk add.**

**Tongkat Ali (Eurycoma longifolia) — AVOID for your goal (likely counterproductive).**
Popular "T-booster," but it works mainly by **lowering SHBG → raising free T**, with only weak aromatase claims. The source's explicit warning: *"in patients on exogenous TRT, lowering SHBG can rapidly increase free testosterone and estrogen conversion."* So for you it could **raise** E2 (more free substrate to aromatize) *and* lift your free T → **nudge Hct up**. Wrong tool — it pushes your two watch-items (E2 and Hct) the wrong way. **Verdict: not for you.** (Boron also lowers SHBG — that's why I flagged Hct monitoring there too — but boron at least has direct E2-lowering data; Tongkat doesn't.)

**Exemestane (Aromasin) — if you go pharma-AI, probably the better choice for *you* than anastrozole.**
Pharma steroidal *suicide* AI (the legit version of arimistane). For your situation it has three advantages over anastrozole: (1) **milder on lipids** — non-steroidal AIs (anastrozole/letrozole) drop HDL ~5–10%, exemestane is lipid-neutral/gentler → matters given HDL is your weakest CV marker; (2) **no estrogen rebound when stopped**; (3) **short half-life = easy to titrate, lower crash risk.** Prescription. **If/when you want real E2 control for pushing test/hCG, exemestane micro-dosed is arguably a better fit than anastrozole for your CV profile.** Discuss with your prescriber.

**Net addition to the hierarchy:**
- **Best clean/sustainable adds:** white button mushrooms (food) + boron (with Hct watch) + leaning out.
- **Avoid:** Tongkat Ali (wrong direction for E2 + Hct), arimistane/6-OXO (grey-market drugs masquerading as supplements).
- **If going pharma:** exemestane > anastrozole for you (lipid-friendly, titratable), micro-dosed PRN.
