## Interaction with TRT (Hct + BP) + Epistaxis Pattern

OSA is not just a sleep issue — it's a shared root cause with two TRT-era problems, now producing acute complications:

### Haematocrit
- Nocturnal hypoxia → EPO drive → erythrocytosis, *independent of and additive to* TRT
- Hct rose 0.45 (pre-TRT) → 0.50–0.51 (on TRT) alongside worsening OSA
- **CPAP compliance is the first-line Hct lever** before any blood donation/phlebotomy
- Effect builds over ~2–3 months of nightly use
- If CPAP doesn't bring Hct down adequately, therapeutic phlebotomy at Red Cross Lifeblood every 2-3 months is the next lever — does NOT require stopping TRT
- TRT preservation pathway: hold dose, CPAP compliance, phlebotomy if needed, dose reduction last resort, stopping TRT almost never required

### Blood pressure
- Untreated OSA independently raises BP
- Baseline BP from memory ~145/80 (stage 1 isolated systolic — common in OSA-driven sympathetic surge)
- Previously 160+ when smoking + drinking heavily, 10kg lighter — lifestyle has already taken ~15 points off
- **Pattern of dangerous exertional/stress peaks** likely in 180-200+ range during gym and post-flight events (inferred from epistaxis triggers)
- **CPAP is first-line for ambient BP** before any antihypertensive medication when BP is stage 1
- **BUT:** given the epistaxis pattern showing dangerous exertional peaks, **ARB consideration alongside CPAP is reasonable** — CPAP needs 2-3 months to retune sympathetic baseline; ARB protects in 2-3 weeks against the exertional surges
- **Most likely first-line medication if added: Telmisartan 40mg daily** — 24+ hour half-life covers exercise BP surges, no impact on exercise capacity (unlike beta-blockers), metabolically neutral, slightly polycythemia-friendly
- Avoid as first-line: beta-blockers (blunt exercise), thiazide diuretics (worsen Hct via volume depletion)
- Decision framework: If BP stage 1 + epistaxis pattern → discuss ARB with GP for training protection. If BP stage 2 (160+/100+) → ARB concurrent with CPAP, not deferred.

**Does his excellent CV-marker profile mean the high BP carries less risk? (assessed 30 Jun / 1 Jul 2026)**
The honest answer splits two ways:
- **Reassuring half (true):** CV risk is largely *multiplicative*. Metabolic-syndrome HT carries 2-4× risk because BP clusters with insulin resistance + dyslipidemia + visceral fat (synergistic sympathetic/RAAS/inflammation). **Shane has none of that cluster** → his **absolute atherosclerotic risk** (MI, plaque-mediated ischemic stroke) is genuinely much lower than a metabolic hypertensive at the same BP. Low ApoB + Lp(a) protect this channel.
- **The half that bites:** the BP itself is **NOT de-risked** by good markers. (Note: the "lean-hypertensive carries enhanced risk" point does NOT apply — he's ~20% BF, not lean — so his risk picture is actually *more favourable + more modifiable* than that: adiposity/deconditioning are reversible contributors. But BP still drives its own channel regardless.)
- **Two decoupled channels:** lipids → atherosclerosis (covered); **pressure → hemodynamic damage (hemorrhagic stroke, LVH, kidney, aortic) is BP-driven largely independent of cholesterol** (every 10 mmHg ↑ raises LV mass; hemorrhagic-stroke risk linear with BP). His markers do ~nothing for this channel.
- **Stroke-specific (his stated priority):** ischemic stroke risk down (good markers); **hemorrhagic stroke risk is BP-driven and NOT covered by his lipids** (low-LDL + high-BP may even interact upward). **His recurrent epistaxis is a real-world canary** — pressure on fragile vessels. Plus 41yo = decades of pressure exposure ahead (cumulative LVH/vascular/kidney toll).
- **Net:** excellent markers genuinely lower absolute atherosclerotic risk (real), but buy **zero slack on BP control** — the pressure-driven risks (esp. hemorrhagic stroke) are fully intact/possibly enhanced in a lean hypertensive. **Reinforces candesartan + CPAP as right, not optional.** Etiology (genetic vs lifestyle) doesn't soften prognosis at a given BP — what differs is what the BP *travels with* (his travels alone = lower total risk; the pressure component itself isn't gentler for being genetic).
