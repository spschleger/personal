## 1. The evidence-based landscape (what's legit)

**PSSE (Physiotherapeutic Scoliosis-Specific Exercises)** is the umbrella term, endorsed by **SOSORT** (Society on Scoliosis Orthopaedic and Rehabilitation Treatment — the international scientific body) and **SRS**. The two leading schools:

- **Schroth method** — the most-researched PSSE. Three-dimensional curve-pattern-specific correction + corrective (rotational) breathing + postural/sensorimotor work. Strong on long-term quality-of-life and trunk-rotation outcomes.
- **SEAS (Scientific Exercises Approach to Scoliosis)** — built on **active self-correction**: training the spine toward its best achievable 3D alignment *without external aids*, then making that correction **automatic/subconscious in daily life**. More neurophysiologic/motor-control framed, emphasises *variability* of stimulus over rigid repetition, and is designed to integrate into daily life and sport with less clinic dependence. **Ranked #1 for Cobb-angle reduction** in the 2024 network meta-analysis.

**Shared core principle across both: active self-correction.** That's the active ingredient — not passive stretching or someone manipulating you.

**2024 network meta-analysis (24 studies, 1,069 subjects):** SEAS best for Cobb-angle reduction; active self-correction best short-term; Schroth best long-term QoL. All beat generic physiotherapy.

**Core stabilization / sensorimotor training** — useful *adjunct*. Head-to-head: Schroth better for the curve itself; core-stability training better for peripheral muscle strength. Emerging RCTs combine core-strength + sensorimotor training specifically for **adult scoliosis low-back pain**.

## 2. Adult-specific evidence (most relevant — Shane is 41, not growing)

Goals shift from the adolescent aim ("stop progression during growth") to: **pain reduction, function, postural control, and slowing degenerative progression.**

**Key adult study (Negrini et al., SEAS-based long-term cohort):**
- 34 adults, mean age 38, mean worst curve **55.8° Cobb** (i.e. real curves, not tiny).
- Dose: **≥2× 45-min sessions/week**, mean ~2 years.
- Result: **68% improved ≥5° Cobb**, mean +4.6° improvement, only 3% progressed.
- Conclusion: SSE "should be considered first-line, especially in patients refusing surgery."
- Mechanism in adults: reduce **postural collapse** → less asymmetric spinal loading → slower degeneration + less pain.

**Takeaway:** exercise genuinely moves the needle in *adults*, not just kids — even with sizeable curves. The lever is consistent active self-correction, not heroics.

## 3. Strength training + scoliosis (directly relevant — lifting-led block)

Consensus from the evidence-based strength world: **resistance training is safe and beneficial** with scoliosis — improves bone density, core/spinal support, and pain.

- **Proof of concept:** world-record powerlifters with significant scoliosis (Lamar Gant — ~5× bodyweight deadlift with severe scoliosis; Heather Connor).
- **Pain-gating rule:** only train when baseline pain ≤4/10; allow ≤1-point rise during a session; address higher daily pain with PT first.
- **Key myth debunked (Progressive Rehab & Strength):** distinguish **technique breakdown** (shows up at higher loads/fatigue — correctable) from **adapted movement** (consistent regardless of load — leave it alone). Asymmetric movement patterns from scoliosis are acceptable if pain-free and consistent. Don't over-pathologise asymmetry.

**The genuine debate to be aware of:**
- *Strength camp* (PR&S, Starting Strength): symmetric barbell training is fine; don't fear loading; don't chase symmetry.
- *PSSE camp* (Schroth/SEAS): curve-specific, often asymmetric/corrective work targeting concave-side musculature.
- **Honest reconciliation:** not actually contradictory. **Lift symmetrically as the foundation** (safe, loaded, builds the whole system) AND do **PSSE self-correction as a separate skill layer**. The corrective work is a motor-control practice, not a replacement for barbell training. Unilateral accessory work (single-arm/leg) is a sensible bridge — good for everyone, and addresses asymmetry without turning lifting into rehab.

## 4. What to AVOID — the noise

- **Commercial chiropractic "scoliosis correction" — CLEAR Institute, ScoliSMART.** Limited-to-no peer-reviewed evidence, for-profit, no standardisation/certification rigour, criticised for marketing comfort-stats over outcomes. Insufficient evidence that chiropractic controls scoliosis progression. **Skip these.**
- **Fear-based "exercises you must NEVER do" lists** (mostly the same chiropractic-marketing ecosystem). For an adult without acute pathology, blanket avoidance of loading/extension is largely outdated.

## 5. Gaps — what's needed before curve-specific prescription

PSSE is **curve-pattern-specific** — it cannot be precisely prescribed without:
1. **Curve details:** location (thoracic / thoracolumbar / lumbar / double-major), **Cobb angle(s)**, convexity direction, rotation — from a recent **standing X-ray**.
2. **A certified Schroth or SEAS physiotherapist assessment** — this is the evidence-based way to get the *correct* self-correction pattern for HIS curve. Wrong-direction corrective work is counterproductive.
3. **Characterise the back pain** — mechanical/curve-related vs disc vs other. (Exercise is first-line for mechanical LBP regardless.)

## 6. Proposed program shape (4 layers — to detail once curve specifics are known)

1. **Lifting (foundation):** the push/pull/legs split. Symmetric barbell work — safe and primary. Add unilateral accessories for balance. Pain-gated (≤4/10 baseline, ≤1-pt rise).
2. **Scoliosis-specific self-correction (the focus):** Schroth/SEAS, assessment-driven. Short daily practice (SEAS-style ~20 min home, or Schroth set). This is the evidence-based corrective layer.
3. **Mobility/flexibility (foundation):** whole-body — T-spine rotation/extension, hips, hamstrings, hip flexors, ankles (relevant to lifting + mining/desk postural load). Include **corrective/rotational breathing** (central to Schroth).
4. **Core / sensorimotor:** anti-rotation + anti-lateral-flexion (Pallof press, loaded carries, side planks) — evidence-supported for adult scoliosis LBP and trunk stability.
