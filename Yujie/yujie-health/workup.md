# Diagnostic Workup

## Principle

She's already partway down the workup pathway (pelvic physio + urodynamics done). The remaining workup needs to subtype the constipation, characterise the structural anorectal state, and confirm/refine the unifying PFD picture across all three domains. The right structure for this is a **multidisciplinary pelvic floor (MDT) clinic** referral, not three siloed single-specialist referrals.

## Records to gather first (no new appointments needed)

1. **Pelvic physio assessment letter** — subtype of PFD, treatment plan, response to date
2. **Urodynamics report** — conclusion line, post-void residual, sensation thresholds, EMG, any noted limitations
3. **Recent bloods** if she's had any
4. **Childhood medical records** if available via her or her parents (paediatrician notes, hospital visits, any imaging)
5. **Current medication list** with start dates (especially hormonal contraception, SSRIs, antihistamines, antimuscarinics)

## GP visit — single combined appointment

Ask for, in one consult:

**Bloods:**
- FBC, electrolytes, calcium, glucose, HbA1c
- TSH (hypothyroidism is a "don't miss" systemic cause of constipation)
- Coeliac serology (tTG-IgA + total IgA)
- Free + total testosterone, SHBG, oestradiol, FSH, LH, prolactin
- B12, vitamin D, iron studies
- AMH if fertility planning is on the table

**Imaging:**
- **Plain abdominal X-ray on a between-BM day (e.g. day 3 of her cycle) with faecal loading score** — single highest-value test for the megarectum / hyposensitivity vs slow-transit question. Cheap, fast, validated (Leech / Barr scoring).

**Exam:**
- **DRE** by a competent clinician on a between-BM day — assess for rectal loading, tone, paradoxical puborectalis contraction, fissure, prolapse, reduced sensation. If GP not comfortable, refer for this.

**Other:**
- Medication review
- Beighton score (60-second hypermobility screen)
- Bladder diary (3 days) and bowel diary in parallel

## MDT referral letter

Single GP referral to a multidisciplinary pelvic floor clinic. The wording materially affects triage speed.

**Suggested wording:**
> 28-year-old female with lifelong severe functional constipation since childhood, including history of childhood retention requiring extended warm baths to induce defecation. Current pattern: 4–6 days between bowel movements with no urge cycle; episodic loose stools punctuating chronic constipation (query overflow / paradoxical diarrhea); visible external anorectal changes including skin tags and signs suggestive of mucosal prolapse. Concurrent urinary incontinence (urodynamic studies previously performed, report attached). Pelvic floor dysfunction confirmed on pelvic physiotherapy assessment. Sexual dysfunction including anorgasmia and reduced genital sensation.
>
> New (July 2026): left-sided electric shock-like pain adjacent to the vaginal opening — spontaneous, relieved by direct pressure, worst when recumbent, sitting and standing equivalent. Neuropathic in quality, pudendal (perineal branch) territory, now coexisting with the longstanding reduced genital sensation.
>
> Requesting multidisciplinary pelvic floor assessment with **anorectal manometry and balloon expulsion test, colonic transit study, MRI defecography, and MRI lumbosacral spine** — the latter to exclude tethered cord / Tarlov cyst / occult dysraphism given the lifelong triad with sensory loss and new unilateral neuralgia whose positional pattern is atypical for pudendal canal entrapment. Gynaecology review for endometriosis if any cyclical pattern is identified.

**Escalation (17 Aug, after bedside checks all positive):** the sentence for the GP is — *"Lifelong bowel and bladder dysfunction, lifelong colder/smaller left leg with a **unilateral left cavus foot and clawed toes**, a skin marker over the sacrum, left toe/heel-walk weakness, and 12 months of progressive left perineal-to-heel pain — I'm concerned about tethered cord / occult spinal dysraphism."* (Lead with the foot — it's the finding a GP can see in five seconds and can't argue with.) Ask for three things: (1) the MRI request amended and **expedited**; (2) a **neurosurgical (spine) referral now, in parallel** — they act on the result and can order further imaging rebatable; (3) the GP to examine and document the neuro signs (reflexes, power, sensation, the sacral marker) so it's on the record. Bring the photos and measurements.

**Also raise with the GP at the same visit:** the recent bowel-habit change (esp. if any leakage without awareness) — that's a neurological sign in this context and the GP should know before, not after, the scan. And ask for a parallel specialist referral (neurosurgery/spine or the MDT pelvic floor unit) — a specialist can request further imaging rebatable and is who acts on a positive finding.

## The three key diagnostic tests

| Test | Question it answers | Where (Sydney) |
|---|---|---|
| **Anorectal manometry + balloon expulsion test** | Dyssynergia? Rectal sensation thresholds? Rectoanal inhibitory reflex present (rules out Hirschsprung's)? | RPA, St Vincent's, Concord |
| **Colonic transit study** (Sitz markers or wireless motility capsule) | Slow-transit constipation? | Major teaching hospitals |
| **MRI defecography** | Rectocele, intussusception, enterocele, perineal descent, dyssynergia in real time | Tertiary radiology centres |

## Conditional further imaging
- **MRI lumbosacral spine — UPGRADED July 2026 from conditional to core test** (now in the referral request above). The new unilateral vulvar neuralgia is a neurological sign; its worst-lying-down pattern is atypical for pudendal canal entrapment, so sacral pathology (tethered cord, Tarlov, occult dysraphism) — the one alternative that unifies the entire picture — can no longer be cheaply dismissed. A clean MRI effectively confirms the peripheral (floor-driven) story by exclusion.
- **Transvaginal ultrasound (deep endo protocol)** if any cyclical symptom pattern
- **Videourodynamics** if urodynamics needs repeating

## Practical sequence

**Week 1–2**: Gather records (physio, urodynamics, any prior). GP visit for bloods, X-ray, DRE, medication review, MDT referral letter.
**Week 2–8**: MDT triage and first appointment. Anorectal manometry / transit / defecography ordered.
**Month 2–4**: Test results back. Diagnosis subtyped. Targeted treatment plan.
**Month 4–12**: Sequenced treatment per `treatment-options.md`.
