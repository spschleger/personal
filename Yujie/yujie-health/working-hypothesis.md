# Working Hypothesis

## Unifying clinical framework

She presents with the **pelvic floor triad**: concurrent bowel, bladder, and sexual dysfunction. The triad fits a single mechanistic explanation because the same muscles (levator ani complex, anal and urethral sphincters), fascial supports, and nerve supply (pudendal nerve, S2–S4) serve all three functions.

**Likely natural history:**
1. Severe early-onset functional constipation with retention behaviour in childhood (warm-bath history is the marker)
2. Chronic rectal distension → **megarectum / rectal hyposensitivity** (stretched wall, blunted afferent signalling, elevated urge thresholds)
3. Decades of straining → secondary **pelvic floor dysfunction** (likely hypertonic or mixed) + external anorectal sequelae (skin tags, possible mucosal/internal rectal prolapse, possible chronic fissure)
4. Pelvic floor dysfunction extends to urinary system → **incontinence** and probably bladder hyposensitivity / dysfunctional voiding
5. Hypertonic pelvic floor + reduced perfusion + chronic guarding → **reduced genital sensation + anorgasmia**

This is a single layered diagnosis, not three coincidental problems.

**Aug 2026 revision:** the accumulating *lifelong, unilateral (left)* findings — cold left leg since childhood, left perineal sensory loss, left continuous perineum-to-heel pain — mean the acquired cascade above may itself sit downstream of a **congenital left-sided sacral / conus lesion** (occult dysraphism / tethered cord). Same layered picture, but with a possible primary cause at the top that the pending MRI can confirm or exclude. Both framings stay live until the report is in.

## Pattern she describes (no urge cycle, "just reaches a point")

Most consistent with **rectal hyposensitivity from chronic distension** rather than classical impaction. In this pattern, defecation isn't urge-driven — it's volume-threshold driven. The threshold is set very high because the rectum has lost normal afferent calibration. The stool may not be hard when it eventually passes; the problem is the timing and sensation, not the consistency. This needs to be confirmed via:
- DRE on a between-BM day
- Plain abdominal X-ray with faecal loading score
- Anorectal manometry with sensation thresholds

## What the hypothesis predicts (testable)

If primary hypothesis is correct, we should see:
- Heavily loaded rectum on between-BM X-ray or DRE
- Elevated sensation thresholds on anorectal manometry
- Dyssynergic pattern on simulated defecation
- Likely normal or borderline slow colonic transit (mostly an outlet/sensation problem, not a motility problem — though both can coexist)
- Defecography showing rectocele, intussusception, or perineal descent ± dyssynergia in real time
- Urodynamics showing some combination of bladder hyposensitivity, dysfunctional voiding, or detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia (mirroring the bowel pattern)

Contradictory findings to watch for:
- Empty rectum on between-BM imaging (would shift toward slow-transit dominant)
- Normal sensation thresholds (would weaken hyposensitivity hypothesis)
- Normal pelvic floor on manometry (would shift toward pure slow-transit or other mechanism)
