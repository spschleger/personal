# MRI day — 18 Aug 2026

## Before you leave (morning)
- [ ] **Call the GP practice at open.** Ask for the MRI request to be amended and re-sent to the imaging practice today (email/fax) with the paragraph below. GP practices do this over the phone routinely; the imaging practice attaches it to the job. If the GP is unavailable, go with the paper note — it works.
- [ ] Print **two copies** of the note below (one for reception to scan into the job, one for the radiographer).
- [ ] Photos on your phone: both feet standing (from behind + from the inside), the sacral skin marker with a ruler.
- [x] Booking confirmed: **MRI lumbar spine + sacrum.** Coverage is right — the conus, filum and full sacral canal will be in the field of view. What's left to secure is the *read*: that the radiologist is asked the tethered-cord / sacral-root questions rather than just the disc question.
- [ ] Her pain is worse lying flat: take whatever analgesia she normally uses beforehand; tell the radiographer she may need a knee bolster or short breaks. Moving mid-sequence ruins the images.

## At reception
Hand in the request **and** the note. Say: *"There's additional clinical information the referring GP didn't include — can you please attach this so the radiologist protocolling the scan sees it, and that the radiologist reporting is asked about the conus, filum and sacral roots."*

## To the radiographer (this is the person who can actually change things on the day)
Say plainly:
> "The GP request says sciatica and incontinence, but the real question is **tethered cord / spinal dysraphism**. She's had bowel and bladder problems her whole life, one leg has been colder and smaller since childhood with a one-sided cavus foot, there's a skin marker over her sacrum, and the pain runs from the vaginal opening down the back of the leg to the heel. Can you please make sure the radiologist knows before protocolling, and that the sacral part of the study is done with the **conus, the filum, and the sacral roots** in mind — and reported that way, not just the discs."

Radiographers can extend coverage and add sequences on the day if the radiologist agrees. They cannot if nobody tells them.

## Before you leave the practice
- [ ] Ask when the report will be ready and confirm it goes to the GP **and** that she can collect a copy herself (she's entitled to it).
- [ ] **Images: ask at check-in, before the scan** — "patient copy of the images please." She's entitled to them (Privacy Act access right). Most practices give a portal/app link within 1–2 days with DICOM download; CD/USB on request, sometimes a small fee — pay it. Specialists can also pull the study via the practice's referrer portal, but having her own copy avoids delays. Key images to look at / share: sagittal T1 + T2 whole lumbosacral spine (conus level, fat in filum), axials L5–S1 and through the sacrum.
- [ ] Ask whether the radiologist saw the note. If they didn't, ask the practice to forward it to the reporting radiologist now.

## When the report arrives — the test
Does it explicitly comment on **conus level**, the **filum**, and the **sacral canal/roots**? If it only talks about discs and says "no significant abnormality", that is a scan that answered the wrong question, not a clean bill. The referring GP can ask the radiologist for an addendum addressing the specific questions — that's normal and free. Then to the neurosurgeon regardless.
