## Read

1. **The panel is a pre-operative workup, not an ED screen.** Coags (PT/APTT/INR) + blood group & antibody screen (group & hold) are what you draw when theatre or surgical admission is on the table — nobody orders a group & hold to send someone home. Neurosurgery was clearly weighing admission/surgery that night before settling on the outpatient pathway. Practical wins: **blood group B Positive, antibody screen negative** is now documented (useful for Beijing — 手术备血, and B is a less-stocked group), and coags are normal (no bleeding-risk obstacle to surgery anywhere).
2. **RPA has now documented, in its own hand, "reduced anal tone and saddle paraesthesia" plus "global left sided deficits in motor function."** Reduced anal tone and saddle paraesthesia are textbook cauda-equina red flags. This sentence, from a tertiary ED with neurosurgery consulted, is the strongest urgency evidence in the file — stronger than anything in the referral letters. Quote it verbatim when chasing Kohan/Wilson and in any Beijing submission (Li Cuicui / Sun Zhenxing).
3. **eGFR 84 at age 29** is the one number to keep an eye on. Normal reporting cutoff is >90 for her age; 84 with creatinine at the top of the female range is not a diagnosis of anything, but in someone with chronic neurogenic bladder and 300 ml residuals it is exactly where early upper-tract strain would first show. Baseline it, repeat with the pre-op bloods, and it strengthens the case that the bladder is not benign-stable. (Single caveat: one-off eGFR wobbles with hydration/muscle mass; trend > point.)
4. **CRP <0.3 and clean FBC** — no systemic inflammation, i.e. no evidence the dermal sinus is currently infected. Good, and worth noting as the "quiet" baseline against the standing ED triggers (fever/redness/discharge at the sacral mark).
