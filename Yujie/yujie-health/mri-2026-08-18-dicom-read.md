## Bottom line

1. **The report is correct but understates the malformation.** "Large lipoma at the cauda equina, tethered cord" is accurate. What the full stacks add: the fat does not stay inside the canal — it passes **out through a posterior sacral bony defect (spina bifida from about S1 down) and is continuous with the subcutaneous fat over the sacrum.** That is the imaging definition of a **lipomyelocele / lipomyelomeningocele-type dorsal spinal lipoma**, i.e. a *closed dysraphism with a skin-to-cord fat bridge*, not a purely intradural "cauda equina lipoma". **[my read — high confidence: seen on SAG T1 Im 12–14 and AX T1 Im 13–24.]**
2. **The lipoma is strongly asymmetric to the LEFT.** At S1 the CSF pocket sits anterior-right, the placode sits at the interface, and fat fills the left half of the canal and runs into the **left lateral recess / S1 foramen** — the report's "encases the left L5 root, compresses the left S1 ganglion". The left-sided roots are therefore the short, fat-embedded ones. This is the anatomical basis for a lifelong *left* leg deficit and cavus foot, and it is the part of the anatomy where a surgeon can do harm. **[high confidence]**
3. **The cord does not end at "L4"; it runs continuously into the fat.** The normal-calibre cord is visible from T12 to L4, then descends posteriorly, flattens into a **placode at L5–S1** and merges with the lipoma. "Conus at L4" in the report is where the cord *starts to look abnormal*, not a true conus. **[high confidence]**
4. **The enhancing S1 lesion is real and sits exactly at the bony defect.** On post-gad fat-sat axials (Se 28001 Im 19–24) and the post-gad sagittal (Se 27001 Im 7–9) there is a patchy, irregular enhancing focus in the dorsal midline subcutaneous tissue at S1–S2, not present on the matched pre-gad fat-sat frames. On non-fat-sat T1 there is a **dark linear streak running from the skin toward the canal fat in the midline at S2–S3** (Se 24001 Im 20–24) and a dark rounded nodule within the fat on the sagittal (Se 18001 Im 14). Together: **dermal sinus tract ± dermoid, inflamed, passing through the dysraphic defect toward the lipoma.** I cannot show the tract entering the dura on 3 mm slices. **[moderate–high confidence for the tract; the dura entry point is not resolvable here]**
5. **No syrinx in the imaged cord (T12 → placode).** Cord cross-sections on the upper and lower lumbar T2 stacks are uniform grey with no bright central cavity. The thoracic and cervical cord are **not imaged** — a whole-spine MRI is still needed before surgery (second tether / syrinx / Chiari). **[high confidence for the imaged segment only]**
6. **No diastematomyelia, no disc disease, no canal stenosis, sacrum formed** (the ala and body are present; the defect is the posterior arch). Agrees with the report.
7. **Bladder was large and full of excreted contrast at 11:27** — incidental, but consistent with the 300 ml residuals on urodynamics. **[low weight]**

## What this means for the surgical debate (Li Cuicui vs Sun Zhenxing)

- **Classification.** By the anatomy this is a **dorsal-to-transitional type lipoma with a skin-to-cord fat bridge through a sacral spina bifida**, plus a **dermal sinus at the same defect**. In Pang's scheme it is dorsal or transitional, not chaotic; in the Xiu Bo / Li Cuicui classification (Front Surg 2024) it would carry several "tethering factors" at once: lipoma, thickened/entrapped roots, dermal sinus, low placode. Their own paper shows outcomes fall as factors accumulate — consistent with Li's "purpose is to halt progression" framing.
- **Why the extent-of-resection question is real here.** The interface between placode and lipoma is broad (the fat occupies half the canal at L5 and most of it at S1) and the **left roots exit through the fat**. Subtotal ("大部") resection is achievable dorsally and in the midline; it is the *left lateral* fat around the L5/S1 roots where aggressive resection risks a new deficit. A surgeon's specific plan for the left L5/S1 roots is therefore the key question — not "how many grams of fat".
- **The dermal sinus changes the operation regardless of resection philosophy.** It must be excised *en bloc from skin ostium to its deep end*, and since it enters at the same defect as the lipoma, the skin incision and dissection are shared. Any surgeon proposing to leave it, or to treat it separately later, is wrong for this anatomy. Ask explicitly.
- **Dural closure / retethering.** A lipomyelocele with a wide defect and a large residual fat surface is the highest-retether configuration. Ask both surgeons what they do to lower it (expansile duraplasty; placode reconstruction; cord–sac ratio target).
- **Whole-spine MRI before the knife.** Not done in Sydney. Beijing will likely re-scan on 3 T anyway; if not, ask for it.
- **Re-tether surveillance will be lifelong** for this type — factor into where follow-up happens (Sydney vs Beijing).

## Dermal sinus — detail (added 29 Aug, late)

**On the images.** Deep end = irregular, partly rim-like enhancing focus in the posterior midline immediately behind the sacral canal at S1–S2 (Se 28001 Im 22–24), absent pre-contrast; a small dark nodule at the same spot inside the fat channel on T1 (Se 24001 Im 21–24). Tract = dark linear streak crossing the ~40–50 mm subcutaneous fat to the skin in the midline ~12–20 mm lower (Se 24001 Im 23–26, ≈ S2–S3), with a small dark focus at the skin — ostium candidate. Orientation (skin opening lower, tract running cranially/inward to the deep end at the bony defect) is textbook for a congenital dermal sinus. **No abscess signs** (no collection, no fat/muscle enhancement, no epidural rim). Enhancement confined to tract/nodule → inflamed tract or small dermoid with inflamed capsule; not separable without DWI. Confidence: deep lesion high (in the report); skin tract moderate.

**What it is.** Epithelium-lined tract from skin inward, from incomplete disjunction of cutaneous from neural ectoderm at wk 3–5 — same event as the lipoma, hence same level/defect. Ends blind, at the dura, or intradurally on cord/filum/lipoma; ~half carry a dermoid/epidermoid at the deep end. Itself a tethering element.

**Why it matters.** (1) Infection route — adult presentation is often meningitis with skin organisms (S. aureus, E. coli, anaerobes), recurrent meningitis, intraspinal/intradermoid abscess. Hers already enhances. (2) Dermoid — slow growth, tether, rupture → chemical meningitis; must be removed with its entire capsule. (3) Surgery — elliptical excision around the ostium, follow the tract through fascia and the defect to the dura, open dura if it enters, remove the deep end completely, same incision as the untethering. Never inject contrast into it.

**Not** a pilonidal sinus or benign coccygeal dimple (those sit in the natal cleft ≤2.5 cm from the anus and don't reach the spine); hers is 2–3 segments higher and leads to an intraspinal lesion.

**Anaesthesia — precise statement.** The untethering itself is under **general anaesthesia** (prone, hours, open dura), run for neuromonitoring: TIVA, no muscle relaxant after intubation, minimal volatile, so triggered EMG / bulbocavernosus / sphincter EMG stay readable — ask Beijing whether their anaesthetists run it that way. What is contraindicated lifelong is **neuraxial anaesthesia for anything else** (labour epidural, spinal for caesarean, epidural steroid): no safe interspace (fat fills the sac from L4/5, placode at L5–S1), the sinus/defect is an infection route, and block assessment is unreliable. Delivery plan = GA if caesarean, IV/nitrous analgesia in labour, obstetric-anaesthesia consult early (`fertility-and-pregnancy.md`). Anatomy stays abnormal after surgery, so this does not expire.

**Practical now.** No squeezing/probing/creams/hair removal at the mark; photograph with a ruler. Same-day ED for fever, headache + neck stiffness, redness/swelling/discharge at the mark, or abrupt change in back/leg pain — in transit and in Beijing ("脊髓栓系 + 皮毛窦，怀疑脑膜炎").
