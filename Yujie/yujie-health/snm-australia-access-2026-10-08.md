# Sacral neuromodulation in Australia: access and funding

Research checked 2026-10-08. Shane reports SNM is being considered for Yujie's bowel and bladder function; no permanent-implant decision or Australian funding approval has been verified.

## Bottom line

SNM is an established specialist treatment in Australia, not an unavailable overseas-only procedure. Continence Health Australia reports approximately 4,500 Australians treated and provision in most major cities; the article is undated and describes older technology, so this is a historical cumulative figure, not a current national prevalence estimate.[1]

A 2025 Westmead conference abstract reports 74 patients assessed from 2016–2024 across public and private hospitals, of whom 54 received full implantation after successful trials. Sixteen had a predisposing neurological or spinal condition. This demonstrates local experience, not a prediction for tethered-cord patients or confirmation of current intake.[2]

## Funding

For Medicare-eligible patients, accepted public-patient surgery in a public hospital is free. This is public hospital funding, not a guarantee that an entire private implant episode is bulk billed. Hospital acceptance, indication, device funding and waiting time must be confirmed.[3]

Private treatment can involve gaps. MBS eligibility is not a whole-package quote; obtain written costs for both stages, device/leads, hospital, anaesthetist, surgeon and follow-up.[3]

Current urinary MBS items 36663 (lead and test stimulation) and 36666 (generator placement) require detrusor overactivity or non-obstructive urinary retention refractory to at least 12 months conservative non-surgical treatment. That is the MBS wording, not proof of a universal public-hospital waiting rule or a requirement to restart a clock after untethering. The specialist must assess the documented preoperative history.[4][5]

Faecal incontinence has its own lead-placement item, 32213. Its wording is not generic bowel dysfunction or constipation. Do not assume constipation/impaired emptying qualifies under a faecal-incontinence item.[6]

## Clinical and practical questions

SNM normally involves a trial before a permanent generator; benefit is assessed before progressing.[7] Generic success figures should not be represented as Yujie's personal odds. Request a neuro-urology/functional-urology review with colorectal input, addressing postoperative recovery, urodynamics, the exact bowel diagnosis, and measured trial goals for each organ.

Ask the proposed public service: Can she receive assessment, the trial, permanent device, programming and later revisions as a public patient? What is the current wait? Which indication is being funded? What documents are needed to establish previous conservative treatment? If implantation is proposed in China, first confirm Australian follow-up/programming support for the exact device and MRI conditions for both generator and lead.
