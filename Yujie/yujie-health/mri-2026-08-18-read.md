1. **Dermal sinus that enhances — *"suggestive of"*, not proven.** A dermal sinus is an epithelium-lined tract from skin inward, left over from development; it may end blindly in subcutaneous fat, or reach the dura, or pass through to the cord/lipoma (often with a dermoid at the deep end). ## Bottom line

1. **The study is not normal, and the abnormality is where the workup predicted it — inside the thecal sac at the lumbosacral junction, dorsally.** On the added L5→sacrum axial stack (T2 Se 23001 and T1 Se 24001, both Im 1/34, level L5) there is a well-defined **intradural soft-tissue structure occupying roughly the dorsal half of a capacious sac**, lying against the posterior dura, with CSF only in front of and beside it. It is **isointense to cord/muscle on both T1 and T2 — i.e. NOT fat, NOT fluid** — and it is bilobed with a thin dark midline cleft. At L5 a normal sac contains only cauda equina roots as scattered dots in CSF; a solid dorsally-attached mass this size is abnormal by any reading.
2. **The radiologist saw it.** After the routine sequences (finished ~11:07) the practice ran a dedicated L5→sacrum stack in T2, T1 and T1 fat-sat, then gave **gadolinium** at 11:27 (axial + sagittal post-contrast). That is a lesion-characterisation add-on — done to separate an enhancing tumour from non-enhancing tissue (low-lying cord, lipoma, cyst). The radiologist also saved a **key image**: the midline sagittal T1 with two measurements (12.12 cm × 1.96 cm) laid along the dorsal lumbosacral canal/soft tissue from L4/5 down to the skin at the coccyx level. Nobody key-images and measures a normal lumbosacral junction.
3. **Two-way differential, and the contrast series (which I can't assess from these frames) is the tie-breaker:**
   - **(A) Tethered cord / closed spinal dysraphism** — a **low-lying conus / terminal placode adherent to the dorsal dura at L5–S1**, ± dorsal lipoma/thick filum. Fits: signal identical to cord; dorsal position hugging the posterior dura (the classic tethered configuration); the wide terminal sac with waisting at L5/S1 and a bulbous end ~S1–2 on the T2 sagittal; the large dorsal fat pad the radiologist measured; the possible cord-like structure still present at L3 on the lower lumbar axial (Se 22001 Im 1/36); and — decisively — the clinical picture: lifelong bowel + bladder dysfunction, lifelong cold/small left leg, **unilateral pes cavus + claw toes**, sacral skin marker, then adult-onset progressive sacral-distribution pain. A tumour does not make a child's leg grow small. **This is my leading read.**
   - **(B) Intradural extramedullary tumour at L5–S1** — myxopapillary ependymoma of the filum, schwannoma, or (less likely at this level/age) meningioma. Fits the 12-month progressive left S1–S3 pain and the recent sphincter change; the bilobed dorsal mass could equally be a tumour. Against: ependymomas and schwannomas are usually T2-bright (this is T2-intermediate); a tumour doesn't explain the congenital limb findings. Not excluded — this is exactly why they gave contrast. **An enhancing mass = B; a non-enhancing cord-signal structure continuous with the conus = A.** Both can coexist (ependymoma arising in a tethered filum is described).
   - Not favoured: disc/foraminal disease (discs T12–L5 look well hydrated with no visible herniation on the frames I have; canal capacious), Tarlov cyst (nothing cystic seen laterally in the sacral canal on the frames available — but I only have one sacral slice), arachnoiditis/clumped roots (no surgery/infection history; a clumped bundle looks like beads, this is a solid mass with sharp margins).
4. **What changes now:** whichever of A/B it is, this is a **neurosurgical lesion**, and the report will almost certainly say so. ## What the radiologist's measurement most likely means

The two lines sit on the dorsal lumbosacral fat/soft tissue between the L4/5 posterior dura and the skin at the top of the natal cleft — the exact place a sacral dimple / dermal sinus / subcutaneous lipoma lives. Three readings, all in the dysraphism family: (i) length × thickness of a **dorsal lumbosacral lipoma** (lipomyelocele picture if it connects to the placode through a posterior arch defect); (ii) a **dermal-sinus-type tract** from the skin marker up to the dura (line 1 = tract length; oblique cranial course is typical); (iii) simply documenting the fat pad relative to the low sac. Any of these makes **the sacral skin marker relevant** — photograph and describe it precisely (dimple / pit / hair / lipomatous swelling / haemangioma; height above anus).
